Master's Thesis · UC3M · 2024–2025

Navigating Cultural Sensitivity in Fashion

Supervised by Dr. Vladimir Melnyk

How does the use of cultural symbolism in fashion branding — framed as appreciation or appropriation — affect Gen Z consumers' brand perception and consumer behaviour?

Mixed-methods study combining Twitter/X social media discourse analysis and a between-subjects experimental survey (Qualtrics, 200+ participants). Data cleaned and coded using thematic discourse analysis and frequency counting in Excel.

Key finding: While cultural appropriation triggers strong moral and emotional reactions in Gen Z, these rarely translate into behavioural change. Cultural proximity, identity salience, and historical marginalisation moderate the relationship between moral emotion and consumer response.

Managerial implication: Leverage grassroots influencers · React fast and transparently · Institutionalize cultural sensitivity · Engage communities directly.

PhD Proposal · UC3M · 2025

Cross-Cultural Ethics in Fashion Branding

Proposed Doctoral Research

Extension of the Master's thesis into a cross-cultural experimental study across multiple clusters: Western European, MENA, East Asian, and Latin American. Examines when and why moral emotions translate into behavioural outcomes — and what brands can do about it.

Design: mixed-method sequential explanatory. Methods: focus groups + Qualtrics experiment. Tools: NVivo, SPSS/R, ATLAS.ti.
